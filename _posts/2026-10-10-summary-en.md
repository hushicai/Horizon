### [Software&\#x27;s Centaur Age May Last Decades](https://seangoedecke.com/softwares-centaur-age-may-last-decades/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · Sean Goedecke · Oct 10, 00:00

**「Background」** The author argues that we are now in a &quot;centaur age&quot; of software engineering, where human engineers paired with AI coding assistants outperform either working alone, and that this partnership could persist for another decade or two.

**「Solution」** He traces the evolution from GitHub Copilot’s autocomplete \(2022\) to chat‑based LLMs, then to supervised agents like Cursor’s agent mode and Claude Code, and finally to largely unsupervised agents after Claude Opus 4.5 \(late 2025\), noting that while agents produce code quickly, their errors are now alignment‑related rather than pure bugs. He weighs reasons the centaur phase might be short \(software’s simplicity relative to chess, massive investment\) against reasons it could be long \(greater total work to be done, spill‑over effects on other fields, historical centaur ages lasting centuries in domains like knitting\). Concluding that chess’s ~20‑year centaur span offers a reasonable default, he advises engineers to stay in the field, deepen the human‑AI partnership, and focus on the shifting human contribution—especially alignment and taste—rather than panic about imminent full automation.

**「Takeaway」** The central thesis is that human‑AI collaboration will remain the dominant mode of software engineering for at least the next decade, so engineers should embrace the partnership, cultivate alignment‑focused skills, and avoid prematurely abandoning their careers.
